How we'll test it

Lab models and animal tissue first; anything swallowed by people waits for supervision and ethics approval.

The bench plan

Each experiment checks one claim the design makes, in the order the claims depend on one another.

  1. 1

    Tune the gel

    Find the poloxamer % that stays liquid when cool and gels near 37 °C (tilt-tube test).

  2. 2

    Make the beads

    Ionic gelation of chitosan into alginate with Mg(OH)₂; check size under a microscope.

  3. 3

    Release test

    Track pH of beads in pH 7 vs pH 2 buffers: quiet at 7, fast rise at 2.

  4. 4

    Adhesion test

    Coat pig esophagus or mucin plates, run simulated saliva over it, measure what stays.

  5. 5

    Acid challenge

    HCl + pepsin on coated vs uncoated tissue; compare damage with Evans blue dye.

Open questions

These are the things we do not know yet, and the tests above are built to find out.

  • Gelling too early. The mouth is warm too. The gel has to set in the throat and esophagus, not on the tongue.
  • Buffer capacity. How much acid one dose can neutralize, and for how many reflux events.
  • Catechol shelf life. Catechol groups oxidize over time, which could weaken the adhesion in a stored bottle.
  • Shellfish-free chitosan. Most chitosan comes from shellfish; an allergy-safe source matters for a drink.

Safety and ethics

All of our testing uses lab models and animal tissue. Nothing is swallowed by a person until there is qualified supervision and ethics approval for it.

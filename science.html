The shield: a gel that sets at body temperature

Poloxamer 407 is a chain with a water-avoiding middle block (PPO) between two water-loving ends (PEO). When the liquid is cool the chains stay apart and it pours like a drink.

At body temperature the PPO blocks gather into micelles, and the micelles pack together into a gel. The drink goes down thin and sets where it is warm.

Staying put on a wet surface

The throat is wet and always moving, which is what makes most coatings slide off. Hyaluronic acid holds water and hydrogen-bonds with mucus. Catechol groups attached to it do what mussel proteins do on a wet rock: they grip.

Together they hold the gel on the mucosa so it can physically block acid and pepsin.

The responders: beads that open in acid

Inside the gel sit chitosan–alginate microspheres loaded with magnesium hydroxide. At normal saliva pH they stay collapsed and hold their buffer.

Chitosan's amine groups have a pKa of about 6.5. As the pH falls they pick up protons and become positively charged. The charges repel one another, the bead swells, and its pores open. Below about pH 5 the buffer is released right where the acid lands.

The twistThe same positive charge makes the beads cling harder to negatively charged mucus. Acid both triggers the buffer and strengthens the coating.

Why magnesium hydroxide

Mg(OH)₂ neutralizes H⁺ without producing CO₂ gas. Carbonate antacids make gas, and a burp can push more reflux up the esophagus.

Raising the pH also slows pepsin, the enzyme that does much of the damage.

Taste

The drink is sweetened with sucralose or stevia and flavored with vanilla or banana, so a child will take it. The flavoring is pH-neutral, so it never triggers the beads in the bottle.

There is no mint. Mint relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter, which is the last thing a reflux product should do.

SentinelGel/Project

Two layers of defense in one drink

SentinelGel is a drink that turns into a sticky gel on the throat and esophagus, then releases a buffer only when reflux drops the pH.

The problem

About 1 in 7 people worldwide live with gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD).

In reflux, stomach contents travel back up. Refluxed stomach acid and the digestive enzyme pepsin injure the throat and esophagus, causing pain, hoarseness and chronic cough.

Normal saliva sits around pH 6.5–7.5. A reflux event can drop the surface of the throat to pH 1–2.

The gap in treatment

Antacids, alginate rafts and proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) work in the stomach. Existing throat coatings are passive: they sit on the tissue as a barrier, and they can't respond to an acid event.

Our answer

One liquid carries a passive shield and active responders, so it protects all the time and reacts during reflux.

Layer 1 · The shield

Thermosensitive adhesive gel

Poloxamer 407 is a thin liquid at room temperature and gels at body temperature. Mussel-inspired catechol–hyaluronic acid makes it stick to wet mucosa and physically block acid and pepsin.

Layer 2 · The responders

Acid-triggered buffer beads

Chitosan–alginate microspheres loaded with Mg(OH)₂ stay closed at normal saliva pH. Below about pH 5 they swell and release the buffer right where acid lands.

The twistChitosan gains positive charge in acid, so the beads also cling harder to the negatively charged mucus at the exact moment of attack. Acid both triggers the buffer and strengthens the coating.

From sip to protection

  1. 1

    Drink it

    Thin, flavored liquid at room temperature.

  2. 2

    It coats

    Warms to 37 °C, gels and sticks to the throat and esophagus.

  3. 3

    Reflux hits

    pH drops below about 5 at the coated surface.

  4. 4

    Beads open

    Mg(OH)₂ neutralizes acid; higher pH slows pepsin.

  5. 5

    Sheds safely

    Leaves over a few hours as surface cells naturally renew.

Where the project stands

SentinelGel is a student concept in development for the Conrad Challenge. The design is built from materials already used in food or medicine, and the test plan sets out how we will check each claim on the bench.

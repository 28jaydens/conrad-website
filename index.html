A throat coating that fights back when acid hits.

SentinelGel is a drink that turns into a sticky gel on the throat and esophagus, then releases a buffer only when reflux drops the pH.

See how it works Try the pH demo

The problem

~1 in 7

people worldwide live with gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD).

Acid + pepsin

Refluxed stomach acid and the digestive enzyme pepsin injure the throat and esophagus, causing pain, hoarseness and chronic cough.

A gap in treatment

Antacids, alginate rafts and PPIs work in the stomach. Existing throat coatings are passive and can't respond to an acid event.

Two layers of defense

One liquid carries a passive shield and active responders, so it protects all the time and reacts during reflux.

Layer 1 · The shield

Thermosensitive adhesive gel

Poloxamer 407 is a thin liquid at room temperature and gels at body temperature. Mussel-inspired catechol–hyaluronic acid makes it stick to wet mucosa and physically block acid and pepsin.

Layer 2 · The responders

Acid-triggered buffer beads

Chitosan–alginate microspheres loaded with Mg(OH)₂ stay closed at normal saliva pH. Below about pH 5 they swell and release the buffer right where acid lands.

The twist

Chitosan gains positive charge in acid, so the beads also cling harder to the negatively charged mucus at the exact moment of attack. Acid both triggers the buffer and strengthens the coating.

  2. 2

The formulation

Every component is used in food or medicine already, or is a modified version of one.

How we'll test it

Lab models and animal tissue first; anything swallowed by people waits for supervision and ethics approval.

1 · Tune the gel

Find the poloxamer % that stays liquid when cool and gels near 37 °C (tilt-tube test).

2 · Make the beads

Ionic gelation of chitosan into alginate with Mg(OH)₂; check size under a microscope.

3 · Release test

Track pH of beads in pH 7 vs pH 2 buffers: quiet at 7, fast rise at 2.

4 · Adhesion test

Coat pig esophagus or mucin plates, run simulated saliva over it, measure what stays.

5 · Acid challenge

HCl + pepsin on coated vs uncoated tissue; compare damage with Evans blue dye.

6 · Open questions

Gelling too early in the mouth, buffer capacity, catechol shelf life, shellfish-free chitosan.
